# Your league, beyond the standings

Your record tells you who won. This site adds context: who is scoring well, who is climbing the rankings, and whose results have benefited from their schedule. Here is how to read each page and chart.

## Start here

Enter your **Sleeper league ID** (the number in your league’s Sleeper URL) and select **Load league**. Anyone can load their own league this way.

- **Through week** sets how far into the season to look. Choosing Week 5 includes the available completed weeks through Week 5, rather than Week 5 alone.
- Your league and week are remembered in this browser as you move between pages and return to the site.
- **Load league** also refreshes your current selection. **Forget** clears the saved league and resets the week selector.
- Use **Power rankings** for the league overview and **Standings & rivalry** for records and schedule comparisons. Click a manager’s name in the power table to open their team page.

## Power rankings: how everyone stacks up

### The summary cards

The cards at the top show the top-ranked manager, the league’s average Power Index, and how many teams are ranked. Joint leaders are shown together; expand **View tied managers** if several teams share first place.

### The Power Index and power table

The **Power Index** combines three views of your team’s performance into a rating from 0 to 100:

| Part of the rating | What it looks at | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scoring production | Your total points over the included weeks compared with the rest of the league | 45% |
| All-play performance | How often your weekly scores beat other teams’ scores, regardless of your actual opponents | 40% |
| Starter projections | Expected points for the starters in the week being rated, using your league’s scoring rules, when complete data is available | 15% |

Higher means stronger performance under this mix of measures. The rating starts from a middle point of 50, with each part moving it up or down. **A rating of 70 does not mean a 70% chance of winning.** These rankings are a way to compare teams within your league, not a guarantee about who wins next.

The small bar beneath each Power Index number is another view of that same rating. It does not represent progress toward a goal.

The **Z-score** columns show how far above or below the league average a team sits in each category:

- **Green, positive:** above the league average.
- **Red, negative:** below the league average.
- **Neutral, zero:** at the average, or every team is equal in that category.
- **N/A for projections:** usable projection data is missing; it does not mean your starters are expected to score zero.

A larger positive number means further above average. These numbers are comparison scores, not fantasy points or percentages. Total scoring measures production across the included weeks; it does not directly measure week-to-week consistency.

Use **Find a manager** to locate someone without changing anyone’s league rank. Teams with equal ratings share a rank. Ratings are compared more precisely than the rounded numbers on screen, so two displayed ratings can look identical while having different ranks.

### Weekly movement: biggest risers and fallers

The **Change** column compares a team’s rank with the previous included week. An up arrow with 3 means the team climbed three places. A down arrow means it fell; a dash means no change.

The strip above the table highlights the biggest risers and fallers, including everyone tied for those spots. **N/A** means there is no comparable previous ranking—for example, it is the first included week, or projection data availability changed between the two weeks.

Movement is relative to everyone else: your rating can improve while your rank falls if other teams improve more.

### Week in review: one week’s scoring

Below the power table, this horizontal bar chart compares every team’s fantasy points for **one completed week**, ordered from highest to lowest.

- Each bar represents one team’s score. Read its position against the points scale.
- The vertical **League average** line shows the average team score for that week. Finishing beyond that line means you outscored the average team.
- **Scoring week** changes this chart without changing the season rankings above it.
- **Highlight manager** makes a selected team easier to spot; it does not change the scores or remove opponents.
- Expand **View exact scores** for full names and precise numbers.

Use this chart to answer, “Was my score actually good this week, even though I lost?”

## Standings & rivalry: results and schedule context

### Actual vs. expected wins chart

This chart shows the difference between the wins you earned against your scheduled opponents and what your scores would earn against randomly chosen league opponents.

**Expected wins looks backward at scores already recorded. It is not a prediction of future wins and does not use player projections.**

Each week, you earn a fraction of an expected win based on how many other teams you outscored. Tied scores count as half a win. Those weekly fractions are then added together.

For example, in a four-team league:

| Week | Your score compared with the other three teams | Expected wins earned |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Higher than two of them | 2 out of 3, or about 0.67 |
| 2 | Higher than one of them | 1 out of 3, or about 0.33 |
| Total | Across both weeks | 1.00 |

If you actually won both games, you have 2 actual wins versus 1 expected win: **+1.00 schedule advantage**.

Here is how to read the chart:

- **Right of the “Even” line, positive:** more wins than your scores would suggest against random opponents.
- **Left of the line, negative:** fewer wins than your scores would suggest.
- **At zero:** actual and expected wins match.
- The distance from zero is measured in wins, not fantasy points. A value of −1.50 means 1.5 fewer wins than expected from those scores.

The table below gives the exact comparison. **H2H games** counts actual scheduled games; **Actual wins + ½ ties** gives half credit for a tied game; **Difference** is the same schedule advantage shown by the bars.

This helps explain a strong-scoring team with a poor record, or a modest-scoring team with a strong record. It describes scheduling outcomes, not manager skill or every form of fantasy luck. Only weeks with a valid scheduled opponent count in this comparison; byes and extra league-median games are excluded.

### Head-to-head record table

**H2H** means head-to-head: your actual results against the opponents you were scheduled to play in the included completed weeks.

| Column | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| W–L–T | Wins, losses, and ties, in that order |
| Win % | Share of those games won, with half credit for ties |
| PF | Points for: your points in those games |
| PA | Points against: your scheduled opponents’ points in those games |

The table is ordered by win percentage, then points scored. Fantasy playoffs, byes, and extra league-median games are excluded, so this can differ from Sleeper’s combined standings.

### All-play record table

All-play asks, “What if I faced every other team each week?” Each higher score earns a win, each lower score a loss, and each equal score a tie.

In a 12-team league, the highest-scoring team goes 11–0–0 for that week—even if it only played one actual game. Those comparisons add up across the included weeks, which is why all-play records contain many more results than H2H records. Win percentage gives half credit for ties.

Use this table to compare scoring performance with less dependence on who happened to be on your schedule. These comparisons do not add wins to your real standings.

### Every-week rivalry

Choose two different managers to see what their record would be **if they played each other in every included week**, regardless of their real schedules.

The displayed W–L–T is from the **first selected manager’s perspective**. A 3–2–0 result means the first manager outscored the second in three weeks and was outscored in two. The points below are each manager’s total across the compared weeks; winning the total-points comparison does not necessarily mean winning more individual weeks.

This is a hypothetical rivalry, separate from both managers’ actual H2H records.

## Your team page: the story behind your rank

Click your name in the power table to see your season in detail.

### The four summary cards

- **League rank:** your position through the included week, with movement from the previous comparable ranking.
- **Power Index:** the same rating shown on the main dashboard.
- **H2H record:** your actual scheduled wins–losses–ties, exactly as defined in the H2H standings. It is not your every-week rivalry record.
- **Expected wins:** the total earned from your weekly score comparisons, as explained above. The schedule advantage below it shows the gap between actual and expected wins.

### Season trajectory: two charts

Both charts run from earlier weeks on the left to later weeks on the right. Each point shows where your team stood **through that week**, including earlier results.

The **upper chart** tracks your Power Index. A rising line means your rating improved under the site’s scoring mix.

The **lower chart** tracks your league rank. **First place is at the top**, so an upward move means a better rank. Your rating and rank can move differently because everyone else’s performance affects your position.

### Scoring by week: your score versus the league average

This chart shows two lines: your fantasy points and the league’s average team score in each week.

Your line above the average means you had an above-average scoring week; below it means a below-average week. The gap shows how far above or below you were. These are individual weekly scores, not season totals, and the average line is not your actual opponent’s score.

Use it to spot strong weeks, slumps, and whether an apparent scoring drop was shared by the whole league.

### What makes up this rating?

This breakdown shows how scoring, all-play results, and starter projections move your rating away from its starting point of 50.

A positive contribution adds to your Power Index; a negative one subtracts from it. For example, contributions of +6 for scoring, +4 for all-play, and 0 for projections produce a rating of 60. The final rating stays between 0 and 100.

These values are **Power Index points**, not fantasy points or extra wins. Small differences when adding the displayed numbers can come from rounding.

### View exact weekly history

Expand this table to see the numbers behind the charts: each week’s rank, Power Index, your score, the league average, and whether projection data was available. Use it when you want precise values instead of reading a line on a graph.

## Data notes: missing weeks and projections

### Why a week might not appear yet

The site includes completed weeks from your league’s regular season, beginning with its configured start week and stopping before its fantasy playoffs. The current NFL week stays excluded until Sleeper advances to the next week, so results may not appear immediately after Monday night’s game.

Choosing a future week does not create results for games that have not happened. If no eligible weeks are complete, the site shows an empty-state message. If a week is missing required team scores, the analysis stops before it rather than leaving teams out of the comparison. Check **Data notes** for the actual cutoff.

### Why “complete starter projections unavailable” appears

Sometimes the available data does not contain usable projections for every team’s starters. When that happens, the site gives **every team a neutral projection contribution** for that week so missing data does not penalize only some managers.

Scoring keeps its 45% weight and all-play keeps its 40% weight. The missing 15% is not handed to those other categories, so ratings generally stay closer to 50. **N/A means unavailable, not a zero-point prediction.** H2H records, weekly scores, and expected wins do not depend on these projections.

### Can past numbers change?

Yes. The site uses currently available Sleeper data, including commissioner score corrections. Those updates can change past scores, records, ratings, and ranks. The charts are a rebuilt view of the season, rather than frozen copies of what the site displayed each week.
